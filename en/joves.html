CAESEN
For young people

You are not alone in this

Studies, friends, social media, family, working out who you are… Feeling bad now and then is human. Here is clear information to help you understand yourself and know who to ask for help.

If you want to hurt yourself right now or fear for your lifeCall 900 20 20 10 (ANAR, free), 024 or 112 now. You can also tell a trusted adult. Right now.

What is normal and what is less so

Everyone has rubbish days: an exam, a row, a disappointment. That happens. It is worth asking for help when, for more than two weeks, any of these happens:

  • You feel sad, empty or irritable almost every day.
  • You have lost interest in things you used to enjoy.
  • You sleep much more or much less than usual, or you have lost your appetite (or eat compulsively).
  • You find it hard to concentrate and your grades or work are slipping.
  • You avoid friends or going out, or you have intense fear of social situations.
  • You have anxiety or panic attacks.
  • You hurt yourself, or you think it would be better not to be here.

If you recognise yourself, it doesn’t mean you are “crazy”. It means you may deserve some support.

How to tell someone

It’s hard, but it’s a very brave step. You can start with:

  • A trusted adult: a parent, guardians, a teacher, your school tutor, a coach, a relative.
  • Your paediatrician or GP (at the CAP). You can book an appointment for what you feel.
  • At school: your tutor or the school counsellor / educational psychologist can help.
  • 900 20 20 10 (ANAR) is free and confidential, for under-18s.

You can say: “I haven’t been feeling well for a while and I don’t know what to do. Can you help me?”. That is enough. More ideas in the “How to talk about it” guide.

Things that can help from today

  • Sleep: regular schedules and phone out of bed. Sleep changes mood a lot. Sleep diary
  • Move: a walk, dancing, sport. A little every day is worth more than a lot on a single day.
  • Breathe and pause: guided breathing or 5-4-3-2-1 when anxiety rises.
  • Putting a name to what you feel: the mood log and the thought diary.
  • Don’t cut yourself off completely: a message to a friend is already a lot.

Social media, screens and pressure

  • What you see on social media is a selection. Don’t compare yourself with other people’s “perfect life”.
  • If a platform or account makes you feel worse, you can mute it, unfollow it or take a break.
  • If someone is harassing you, threatening you or sharing photos of you, it is not your fault. Keep screenshots, block them and tell an adult. You can call ANAR (900 20 20 10).

If you are a friend of someone who is struggling

  • Listen without judging and without minimising (“it’s not that bad”).
  • Ask directly and calmly how they are. Asking doesn’t make anything worse.
  • Don’t keep it secret if someone wants to harm themselves: tell an adult. It isn’t betraying them: it’s caring for them.
  • If there is immediate danger, don’t leave them alone and call 112.
