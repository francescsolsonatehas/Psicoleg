CBT · self-help

Behavioural activation, sleep diary and panic log

Three cognitive behavioural therapy tools for depression, insomnia and anxiety. Stored only in your browser.

A support, not a treatmentIf you have felt unwell for weeks, have had repeated panic attacks or have not slept well for months, talk to your doctor or a psychologist. Find help

🌱 Behavioural activation (depression)

When you are feeling low, the urge is to do less, and that makes mood worse. The idea is the opposite: act first, and motivation will follow. Plan small activities, then note how much pleasure and sense of achievement they really gave you.

Your activities

    TipsStart with things that take 5-10 minutes. Pick some that give you pleasure and others that give you a sense of achievement. Don’t wait until you feel like it: plan the moment (day and time). If low mood lasts for weeks or you have thoughts of harming yourself, get help now: 024 or 112.

    🌙 Sleep diary

    Note it down every morning (2 minutes). With one or two weeks of data, you and the professional can spot patterns.

    Yesterday…

      Sleep hygieneRegular schedule (weekends too) · daylight in the morning · less caffeine after noon · no alcohol to get to sleep · screens out of bed · if you don’t fall asleep in about 20 minutes, get up and do something calm until you feel sleepy. The bed is for sleeping. For persistent insomnia, CBT-I is the recommended treatment: read the sheet.

      💨 Panic log

      During a panic attackThe symptoms are very unpleasant but not dangerous, and they pass: they usually peak within about 10 minutes. Breathe out slowly (in for 4 seconds, out for 6), let the sensation pass without fighting it and remind yourself: “this is panic, it will pass”. If it is your first time or you have chest pain that doesn’t go away, call 112.

      Note it down afterwards, once you are calmer. Seeing the pattern helps reduce the fear.
