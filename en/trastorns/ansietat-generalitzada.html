Anxiety and related

🌊 Generalised anxiety disorder

Excessive worry that is hard to control, on most days, for months.

Information, not a diagnosisThis page is for information only. Only a professional can assess your situation.

What it is

Everyone feels anxious from time to time. When worry is constant, out of proportion and gets in the way of work, relationships or rest, it may be generalised anxiety disorder (GAD).

Common symptoms

  • Constant worry about many things (health, work, family, money)
  • Restlessness, feeling “on edge”
  • Tiredness and difficulty concentrating
  • Muscle tension, headaches or digestive problems
  • Difficulty falling asleep or unrefreshing sleep
  • Irritability

Myths and facts

Myth: You just need to “stop thinking about it”.
Fact: Anxiety is not a matter of willpower. There are effective treatments and skills that can be learned.
Myth: Anxiety is always bad.
Fact: It is a natural response that protects us. It becomes a problem when it is intense, constant and out of proportion.

When to see a professional

If worry takes up much of your day, you find it hard to function normally, or it has lasted more than six months, talk to your family doctor or a psychologist.

What treatments do clinical guidelines recommend?

Clinical guidelines recommend cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) as the first option; it teaches ways to manage worry. In some cases a professional may also consider medication. The decision is always up to a health professional.

Tips for family and friends

  • Listen without minimising (“it’s not that bad”).
  • Avoid agreeing with every fear, but don’t push them to face everything at once either.
  • Encourage them to ask for help and offer to go with them to the first appointment.
