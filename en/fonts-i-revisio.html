Transparency

Who reviews it and where the information comes from

A mental health website should be able to explain where what it says comes from. Here is, without sugar-coating, how it was made and what is still pending.

Professional review

PendingThis website has not yet been reviewed by any licensed mental health professional. Until it is, treat all content as general information and always consult a professional.

Once there is a professional reviewer, the condition sheets, the tools and questionnaires, the phone numbers and resources, and the translations will be reviewed. Each sheet and guide will say whether it has been reviewed and when.

How the content was produced

  • The texts were written with the help of an artificial intelligence tool, based on the public sources listed below. They are a popular-science summary: they contain no original research or clinical experience.
  • The Spanish and English versions are translations made with the same tool, without review by a translator or a professional.
  • The PHQ-9 and GAD-7 questionnaires are adaptations of the original scales, which are free to use, and are not officially validated versions in any of the three languages.
  • The self-help tools are based on well-known cognitive behavioural therapy techniques. They are a support, not a treatment.

Reference sources

  • World Health Organization – Mental health
  • Canal Salut (Government of Catalonia)
  • NICE – Mental health clinical guidelines
  • GuíaSalud – Clinical practice guidelines of the Spanish National Health System
  • ICD-11 (WHO) and DSM-5-TR (American Psychiatric Association), as reference classifications
  • Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JBW. The PHQ-9: validity of a brief depression severity measure. J Gen Intern Med. 2001;16(9):606-613.
  • Spitzer RL, Kroenke K, Williams JBW, Löwe B. A brief measure for assessing generalized anxiety disorder: the GAD-7. Arch Intern Med. 2006;166(10):1092-1097.

Updates and errors
