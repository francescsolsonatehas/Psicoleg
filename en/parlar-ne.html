How to talk about it

Putting words to what is happening to you is a big step. Here are ideas and phrases to start with family, your GP and at work.

It doesn’t have to be perfectYou don’t need to have it all figured out or find the exact words. You can start by just saying “I’m not feeling well”.
With family and friendsWith your doctorAt work

With a relative or friend

Before you start

  • Pick someone you trust, not necessarily the first person who comes to mind.
  • Pick a calm moment, without rush or distractions. It can be while walking or by message, if face to face is hard.
  • Decide what you want to ask for: listening, company, help finding a professional…

Phrases to start with

  • “I haven’t been feeling well lately and I wanted to tell you. I don’t need you to fix it, just to listen.”
  • “I’ve had trouble sleeping for a while and I’m very anxious. I’d like to ask for help and it would help if you came with me.”
  • “I don’t quite know what’s wrong, but I don’t feel like myself. Can you help me find who I can ask?”

If the reaction isn’t what you expected

Sometimes people downplay it (“that’s just you”) or get scared. Often it isn’t a lack of affection, they just don’t know what to say. You can tell them: “It hurts to hear that; what helps me is…”. And remember you can try someone else or go straight to a professional.

With your GP (CAP)

The family doctor is the usual way into the public system. They have little time, so preparing helps a lot.

What to bring

  • A list of what you feel, since when and how it affects you (sleep, work, relationships, appetite).
  • Any medicines or supplements you take.
  • Family medical history, if you know it.
  • Records from this website (mood, sleep, panic…): you can print or download them.

How to start the appointment

State the main reason right away, in one sentence. For example:

  • “For a few weeks I’ve been feeling very low and without energy, and it’s hard to go to work.”
  • “I have panic attacks and I’m avoiding more and more things.”
  • “I’ve been sleeping very little for months and I’m exhausted.”
If you are thinking of harming yourselfSay it out loud. It will be treated as a priority and you won’t be judged. If it is urgent, call 024 or 112.

What can happen

  • They will ask about your symptoms and, sometimes, give a short questionnaire.
  • They may ask for a blood test to rule out physical causes (for example thyroid problems or anaemia).
  • They may propose follow-up, treatment or a referral to a psychologist or a mental health centre (CSMA).

Useful questions

  • “What options do I have? What are the pros and cons of each?”
  • “How long will it take before I notice an improvement?”
  • “When shall we meet again?”
  • “If I get worse, what do I do or who do I call?”

You can bring someone with you. If you don’t feel listened to, you have the right to ask for another appointment or a second opinion.

At work

Deciding whether to tell your employer, and who, is your decision. You are not obliged to disclose your diagnosis to your employer.

If you need sick leave

  • Your doctor handles the sick leave. The sick note that reaches your employer does not include the diagnosis.
  • You can simply say: “My doctor has signed me off for health reasons”.

If you want to keep working with adjustments

You can ask for reasonable changes: flexible hours, working from home some days, sharing out tasks, breaks, or reducing a temporary workload. Some example phrases:

  • “I’m going through a difficult time with my health and I’d like to talk about some temporary adjustments so I can keep working.”
  • “A more regular schedule would help me for a few weeks.”

Who to talk to

  • Your direct manager or human resources, if you trust them.
  • The company’s occupational health and safety service, which has a duty of confidentiality.
  • Union or safety representatives, if you have them.
  • If the distress comes from work (harassment, overload, discrimination), note down dates and facts and ask for advice.
Your health mattersLooking after yourself is not failing at work. If you can’t go on, talking to a professional is the first step.
