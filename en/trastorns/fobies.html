Anxiety and related

🕷️ Specific phobias

Intense, out-of-proportion fear of a specific thing or situation (animals, heights, blood, flying...).

Information, not a diagnosisThis page is for information only. Only a professional can assess your situation.

What it is

A specific phobia is a very intense fear of a particular object or situation that almost always appears on encountering it and is clearly out of proportion to the real danger. The person avoids it or endures it with great distress. They are among the most common fears and respond best to treatment.

Common symptoms

  • Immediate fear or distress when faced with the trigger (or even just thinking about it)
  • Palpitations, sweating, trembling, feeling breathless or dizzy
  • Avoiding situations (flying, driving, going to the dentist, seeing blood...)
  • Planning life so as not to meet what is feared
  • Knowing the fear is exaggerated and still being unable to control it

Myths and facts

Myth: It is an exaggeration or a lack of willpower.
Fact: Fear is an automatic response of the body. It is not solved by “trying harder”, but it is by gradual exposure.
Myth: If I face it all at once, it will go away.
Fact: Sudden exposure without preparation can reinforce the fear. It works better when gradual and agreed in advance.

When to see a professional

When avoidance limits work, studies, health (e.g. not going to the doctor) or relationships, or when the fear causes you significant distress.

What treatments do clinical guidelines recommend?

The treatment of choice is cognitive behavioural therapy with gradual exposure, often in just a few sessions. Medication is not usually the first resort.

Tips for family and friends

  • Don’t make fun of them or compare it to your own fear: for them it is real.
  • Don’t force them to face it all at once, but don’t help them avoid everything either.
  • Celebrate small steps forward and respect the pace of exposure.
