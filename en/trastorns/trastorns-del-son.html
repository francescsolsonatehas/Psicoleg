Sleep

😴 Other sleep disorders (apnoea, sleepiness, nightmares)

It isn’t all insomnia: apnoea, hypersomnia, narcolepsy, nightmares and parasomnias also affect rest.

Information, not a diagnosisThis page is for information only. Only a professional can assess your situation.

What it is

Besides insomnia, there are other sleep problems worth knowing about. Obstructive sleep apnoea makes breathing stop briefly during the night. Hypersomnia and narcolepsy cause excessive daytime sleepiness. Repeated nightmares can appear with stress or trauma. Parasomnias (sleepwalking, night terrors) are more common in childhood. Many have a physical cause that needs to be assessed.

Common symptoms

  • Tiredness or sleepiness during the day despite sleeping many hours
  • Loud snoring, pauses in breathing or waking up gasping
  • Morning headaches, dry mouth
  • Frequent nightmares that cause distress or make you avoid sleep
  • Unusual movements or behaviour during sleep (walking, talking, shouting)
  • Uncontrollable sleep attacks during the day

Myths and facts

Myth: Sleeping little is a sign of weak will or of being “lazy”.
Fact: Sleep depends on health and physiology. Extreme tiredness can be a medical sign.
Myth: Nightmares are just the result of a heavy dinner.
Fact: They can be linked to stress, trauma or medication, and they can be treated.

When to see a professional

If daytime sleepiness makes it hard to drive, work or study, if someone sees you stop breathing while you sleep, or if nightmares or night-time episodes are frequent.

What treatments do clinical guidelines recommend?

It depends on the disorder. Apnoea is treated with lifestyle measures and, sometimes, positive-pressure devices (CPAP). A sleep study may help. For nightmares there are techniques such as imagery rehearsal therapy. A medical assessment is needed; sleep hygiene alone is not enough.

Tips for family and friends

  • If you notice pauses in breathing or loud snoring, encourage them to see a doctor.
  • Avoid waking a person with parasomnias abruptly; protect them from bumps.
  • Don’t joke about tiredness: it can be medical.
