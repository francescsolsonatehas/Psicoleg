Anxiety and related

🩺 Health anxiety (hypochondria)

Persistent worry about having or being able to develop a serious illness, despite medical checks.

Information, not a diagnosisThis page is for information only. Only a professional can assess your situation.

What it is

Health anxiety is when normal body sensations are interpreted as signs of a serious illness and the worry continues even though tests are normal. It is not “pretending to be ill”: the distress is real. It often leads to constant checking, searching online or asking for many appointments, or else to avoiding everything.

Common symptoms

  • Constant fear of having or developing a serious illness
  • Excessive attention to bodily sensations (heartbeat, aches, marks)
  • Checking your body, measuring vital signs or looking up symptoms online
  • Repeatedly asking relatives or doctors for reassurance
  • Avoiding news, hospitals or conversations about illness
  • Brief relief after a check-up, then the fear returns

Myths and facts

Myth: It’s imagination: you don’t really feel anything.
Fact: The sensations are real. The problem is how they are interpreted and the fear they cause.
Myth: One more test will end the fear.
Fact: Reassurance wears off quickly and feeds the cycle of checking. That is why treatment works on reducing it.

When to see a professional

When the worry takes up a lot of time, causes distress, interferes with daily life or leads to many visits or tests without relief.

What treatments do clinical guidelines recommend?

CBT is the best-evidenced treatment: reinterpreting sensations, reducing checking and reassurance-seeking. It can be combined with coordination between the family doctor and mental health services. An appropriate medical check is never ruled out.

Tips for family and friends

  • Listen without ridiculing, but avoid giving repeated reassurance: it reinforces the problem.
  • Encourage them to have a single doctor they see regularly.
  • Suggest activities that distract them and give them a full life.
