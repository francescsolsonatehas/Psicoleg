Risk and crisis

🤝 Self-harm and suicidal thoughts

If you are thinking of harming yourself right now, you are not alone: ask for help now.

If you are in danger nowCall 112 or 024 (free, 24 h). You don’t have to face this alone.
Information, not a diagnosisThis page is for information only. Only a professional can assess your situation.

What it is

Suicidal thoughts and self-harm are forms of very intense emotional pain. They are more common than people think and they can be treated. You can feel better, even if it doesn’t seem so right now.

Common symptoms

  • Feeling there is no way out or being a burden
  • Thoughts of death or of wanting to disappear
  • Saying goodbye, giving away belongings, sudden changes in behaviour
  • Self-harm as a way of coping with emotional pain

Myths and facts

Myth: Talking about it gives people ideas.
Fact: Asking directly and calmly helps and does not increase the risk.
Myth: People who talk about it don’t do it.
Fact: Every comment must be taken seriously.

When to see a professional

If right now you are thinking of harming yourself or ending your life, call 112 (emergencies) or 024 (suicide prevention line, free, 24 h) now. If you can, don’t stay alone.

What treatments do clinical guidelines recommend?

Urgent or priority professional assessment, a safety plan, psychotherapy (CBT, DBT) and, if needed, medication. Recovery is possible.

Tips for family and friends

  • Ask directly and calmly: “Are you thinking of harming yourself?”.
  • Listen without judging and don’t promise to keep it secret.
  • Don’t leave them alone if there is immediate risk, and call 112.
