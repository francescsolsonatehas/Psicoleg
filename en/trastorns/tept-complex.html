Trauma and grief

🧩 Complex PTSD and ongoing trauma

Effects of repeated or prolonged trauma, often from childhood: beyond memories, it affects how you see yourself and relate to others.

Information, not a diagnosisThis page is for information only. Only a professional can assess your situation.

What it is

Complex trauma refers to repeated or prolonged experiences that are hard to escape (abuse, neglect, violence). In addition to PTSD symptoms, ICD-11 describes complex PTSD with difficulty regulating emotions, a negative self-image and problems maintaining close relationships.

Common symptoms

  • Intrusive memories, nightmares or flashbacks
  • Avoiding people, places or conversations that recall what happened
  • Very intense emotions that are hard to calm, or feeling emotionally numb
  • Persistent feelings of shame, guilt or of being “defective”
  • Difficulty trusting others and keeping close bonds
  • Feeling disconnected from your body or from reality (dissociation)

Myths and facts

Myth: If it happened years ago, I should be over it by now.
Fact: Trauma doesn’t follow a calendar. Symptoms can appear or get worse many years later.
Myth: If it had been that serious, I would remember everything clearly.
Fact: It is common for memories of trauma to be fragmented or confused. That does not invalidate the experience.

When to see a professional

If the past shapes your present (relationships, sleep, emotions, substance use) or if your current safety is at risk. Look for a professional trained in trauma.

What treatments do clinical guidelines recommend?

A phased approach is recommended: first safety and stabilisation (emotion regulation, calming skills), then processing the trauma with trauma-focused therapies (trauma-focused CBT, EMDR) and, finally, reintegration into daily life. The pace is set by the person.

Tips for family and friends

  • Believe them and don’t press for details: they choose when and what to share.
  • Help them feel safe through consistency and predictability.
  • Learn about trauma: intense reactions are not “being dramatic”.
